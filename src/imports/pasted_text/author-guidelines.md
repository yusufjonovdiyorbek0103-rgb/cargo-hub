Review Article

Technical Note
Description:

Short Communication
Description:

Recommended length: 2,000–4,000 words.

Perspective / Policy Paper

Presents a scholarly argument, policy analysis, or expert perspective supported by academic literature and evidence.

PAGE 4: PEER REVIEW POLICY

Page title:
Peer Review Policy

Hero subtitle:
CAJAIDT applies a double-blind peer-review process to support scholarly quality, fairness, and editorial integrity.

Section 1: Review Model

Use this text:
CAJAIDT uses a double-blind peer-review model. The identities of authors and reviewers are concealed during the review process. Manuscripts are evaluated based on originality, relevance to the journal scope, methodological quality, clarity, ethical compliance, and contribution to the field.

Section 2: Review Workflow

Create a visual workflow:

1. Submission
2. Technical Check
3. Editorial Screening
4. Reviewer Invitation
5. Double-Blind Review
6. Editorial Decision
7. Revision
8. Final Decision
9. Copyediting and Publication

Section 3: Reviewer Criteria

Use cards:
- Subject expertise
- Academic or professional experience
- Absence of conflict of interest
- Ethical reliability
- Ability to provide constructive feedback
- Timely response

Section 4: Editorial Decisions

Create decision cards:
- Accept
- Minor Revision
- Major Revision
- Reject and Resubmit
- Reject

Section 5: Review Timeline

Use this text:
The journal aims to complete initial technical screening within 5–7 working days. External peer review usually takes 2–4 weeks, depending on reviewer availability and manuscript complexity. Revised manuscripts are assessed by editors and, when necessary, returned to reviewers.

Section 6: Confidentiality

Use this text:
Reviewers must treat manuscripts as confidential documents. Manuscript content, data, figures, methods, and findings must not be shared, used, or disclosed before publication.

Section 7: Conflicts of Interest

Use this text:
Editors and reviewers must declare any actual or potential conflicts of interest. A reviewer should decline a review invitation if there is a personal, institutional, financial, competitive, or collaborative relationship that could affect impartiality.

PAGE 6: AI USE POLICY

Page title:
AI Use Policy

Hero subtitle:
Guidelines for responsible disclosure and use of artificial intelligence tools in manuscript preparation and research.

Section 1: General Principle

Use this text:
Authors may use AI-assisted tools for language editing, formatting assistance, coding support, or analytical support when appropriate. However, authors remain fully responsible for the accuracy, originality, integrity, and ethical compliance of the submitted work.

Section 2: AI Tools Cannot Be Authors

Create a highlighted rule card:
AI tools, chatbots, language models, or automated systems cannot be listed as authors. Authorship is limited to individuals who can take responsibility for the work, approve the final manuscript, and respond to questions about accuracy and integrity.

Section 3: Disclosure Requirement

Use this text:
If AI tools were used in preparing the manuscript, authors must disclose the tool name, purpose of use, and the parts of the manuscript or research process affected. Simple grammar or spelling correction does not normally require detailed disclosure, but substantial AI-assisted writing, analysis, coding, image generation, or data processing must be disclosed.

Section 4: Prohibited Uses

Create warning cards:
- Generating fake data
- Creating fabricated references
- Producing misleading results
- Replacing human scholarly judgment
- Uploading confidential reviewer comments into public AI systems
- Creating images or figures without proper disclosure
- Using AI-generated text without verification

Section 5: Suggested Disclosure Statement

Create a sample statement box:
During the preparation of this manuscript, the authors used [tool name] for [specific purpose]. The authors reviewed, edited, and verified all AI-assisted outputs and take full responsibility for the content of the manuscript.

Section 6: Reviewer Use of AI

Use this text:
Reviewers must not upload confidential manuscripts, unpublished data, or review materials into external AI tools unless explicitly allowed by the journal and the tool meets appropriate confidentiality standards. Reviewers remain responsible for the content and quality of their review reports.

Page title:
Plagiarism Policy

Hero subtitle:
CAJAIDT screens submissions for originality and takes plagiarism, self-plagiarism, and text recycling seriously.

Section 1: Originality Requirement

Use this text:
Submitted manuscripts must be original and must not contain plagiarized, fabricated, or improperly reused content. Authors are responsible for ensuring that all sources are properly cited and that submitted work does not violate academic integrity standards.

Section 2: Types of Plagiarism

Create cards:
- Direct plagiarism
- Mosaic plagiarism
- Self-plagiarism
- Duplicate publication
- Uncited paraphrasing
- Data or image plagiarism
- Reference manipulation

Section 3: Similarity Screening

Use this text:
All submissions may be checked using plagiarism detection tools during technical screening or at later editorial stages. Similarity reports are interpreted by editors, taking into account quoted material, references, methodology descriptions, and legitimate overlap.

Section 4: Editorial Actions

Create a decision flow:
Minor overlap → Author clarification or revision
Significant overlap → Rejection before review
Serious plagiarism → Rejection and possible institutional notification
Post-publication plagiarism → Correction, expression of concern, or retraction

Section 5: Author Responsibility

Use this text:
Authors should properly cite all sources, avoid copying text from previous publications, clearly identify reused materials, and ensure that all co-authors have reviewed the manuscript before submission.
